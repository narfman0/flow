# Narrative — flow

## Logline

In 2055, four strangers are independently pulled toward an abandoned physics facility by a signal
they feel rather than hear. What they find isn't a disaster to stop — it's an ending already in
progress, and the only question is who you want beside you when it completes.

---

## World

**Setting**: Near-future Earth, ~2055. Climate adaptation infrastructure is everywhere — sea walls,
vertical farms, rerouted rivers. AI handles most logistics. The world is recognizable but tired,
pragmatic, a little beautiful in its patchwork fixes.

**The event**: In 2038, a privately-funded quantum physics consortium called the **Veritas Group**
ran an experiment at a remote facility in the mountains. Project Meridian was designed to prove
that closed timelike curves — regions of spacetime that loop back on themselves — were
theoretically impossible at any observable scale.

They were wrong.

The experiment created a small CTC bubble: a region of spacetime moving in the opposite temporal
direction from the rest of the universe. The boundary between the two orientations produced
immediate, localized geometry distortions — brief moments where the walls of the facility briefly
breathed inward, where hallways seemed longer than their dimensions, where two rooms briefly
occupied the same space.

The consortium filed an internal incident report, sealed the affected wing, and quietly shut down
that research line. No one used the words "timelike curve" in writing. The world never knew.

**The facility now**: The Veritas Group still operates the site — skeleton crew, legitimate physics
research in other wings. A small government agency (quiet, understaffed, no official name in any
public document) has been monitoring the sealed wing for 17 years. They know something is there.
They don't know what. They've been telling themselves it's probably fine.

**The bubble**: It has been contracting since 2038, spiraling back toward its origin point — the
moment of the experiment. As it compresses, its boundary (the source of all geometric distortion)
moves inward. The anomalies — brief, beautiful breaks in geometry — have been gradually converging
on the facility for years. The bubble is in its final months.

**The signal**: The contracting bubble emits two harmonics. The primary harmonic is a low-frequency
geometric distortion that propagates outward — most people register it as a vague feeling, a pull,
a sense that something important is in a direction they can't name. Some people feel it more
strongly than others. The secondary harmonic is relational: it pulls people toward others who are
already feeling the primary. The rivals aren't chasing the signal. They're chasing the person
they're connected to, drawn without quite understanding why.

## Characters

### Main Party

**KAI** — acrobatic courier, late 20s. Fast, fluid, never still. Runs on instinct and momentum.
Afraid, underneath it, that nothing he does actually matters — so he never gives himself time to
think about it. Feels the signal as a physical pull, like gravity in the wrong direction. Has been
unconsciously rerouting his courier runs toward the mountains for months before he admits it.

**SELA** — analyst, early 30s. Sharp, adaptable, a different version of herself in every room.
Has spent so long reading people and adjusting to them that she's not sure who she is when no
one's watching. Feels the signal as a sound just below hearing — a note that makes her want to
stop performing. Kai is the first person she doesn't automatically calibrate herself for. She
falls for him because of that, not despite it.

**DAVEN** — former physicist, late 50s. Dry, spare with words, seems to have made peace with
something difficult. He hasn't. Was a junior researcher at the Veritas facility in 2038. Didn't
witness the experiment but processed the incident report data. Understood exactly what the numbers
meant, was told to forget it, and left physics entirely. Has been watching the anomalies for 17
years from a distance. Knows more than he admits until it becomes impossible not to say.

**PELL** — youngest, mid 20s, treats everything like a field trip. Manic wonder, inexhaustible
curiosity, never seems scared. The fearlessness is real and it is also a wall. Feels the signal
as color — perceives it visually as a shimmer at the edge of vision pointing in a direction.
Has been chasing the shimmer for weeks, framing it as an adventure, refusing to ask what it means.

### Allies (Apparent Enemies)

**RENN** — rival courier who keeps outrunning Kai and stealing his jobs. Quick, unpredictable,
laughs at everything. Has secretly been taking the jobs Kai would have failed, covering for him
without telling him. Drawn to Kai by the secondary harmonic, frames it as professional competition.
Becomes his most chaotic, loyal friend.

**MARSH** — researcher who appears to be investigating Sela, following her and taking notes. Is
actually writing a book about extraordinary people and finds her the most interesting subject she's
ever encountered. Deeply awkward, completely earnest. Drawn to Sela and cannot explain the pull.
Tries to be scientific about it.

**COLE** — Daven's former colleague and closest friend, with whom he had a serious falling out
years ago. They argue constantly, bicker like an old married couple, and are completely loyal to
each other underneath the noise. Cole has been feeling the signal longer than anyone. Has been
living near an anomaly site for years. Has not mentioned this to Daven because they weren't
speaking. Dies near the end — not violently; quietly, in the middle of an argument, when the
bubble closes and something in him that was connected to its boundary goes still. Daven has to
finish the argument alone.

**IVO** — a committed skeptic who shows up to debunk everything Pell is excited about. Gets
proven wrong. Repeatedly. Becomes a true believer and is subsequently more obnoxious about it
than Pell ever was. Drawn to Pell without explanation, frames it as a need to document and
correct the record.

## Act Structure

### Act 1 — Convergence

Four short opening vignettes. Each character in their own world, each experiencing the signal
differently. Each makes a decision to follow it without fully understanding why.

They arrive at the same mountain town — a small settlement near the old Veritas facility — within
days of each other. They meet badly. Suspicion, misread intentions, wrong first impressions.
Renn is already there and immediately antagonizes Kai. Marsh is taking photos of Sela across the
town square. Ivo introduces themselves to Pell by explaining why everything Pell just said
is wrong.

Daven arrives last, sees the facility on the horizon, and says nothing.

The act ends when a geometry anomaly occurs in the town itself — the first one outside the
facility boundary — and they're all present for it. Brief, beautiful, unsettling. The walls
of the inn breathe inward for eight seconds. Then nothing.

They agree, uneasily, to keep moving together.

### Act 2 — Character Chapters

One chapter per main character. The party travels to a location tied to that character's backstory
but finds it distorted — not destroyed, not dangerous, just *wrong in a specific way* that reflects
the character.

Each chapter ends with a wall effect moment: the geometry punctuation beat. Something shifts in
the character, and the world briefly acknowledges it.

**Kai's chapter**: His home city. Certain routes loop — he's been running them his whole career
and suddenly they don't add up. Solving the loop requires him to stop moving and actually look at
where he is. Renn appears midway through. They argue, then work together. At the end, Kai admits
out loud that he's scared. The wall effect hits. The loop resolves.

**Sela's chapter**: A conference facility where she gave her first major presentation years ago —
a moment she remembers as the first time she performed a version of herself for an audience.
The building is duplicated: two identical floors occupying the same space, each populated by
slightly different versions of the same people. Marsh appears, still taking notes. Eventually
helps Sela find which version of the floor is real by asking her which choice felt like *her*.
The wall effect when she steps onto the real floor for the first time.

**Daven's chapter**: The outskirts of the Veritas facility — not the sealed wing yet, but the
grounds he knew in 2038. He finds his own incident report, framed and mounted in a maintenance
corridor. Someone preserved it. Cole appears. They argue about why Daven never came back. Daven
tells the truth about what the report said and what he chose to do with it. The wall effect.
Cole is quiet for the first time in the game.

**Pell's chapter**: An anomaly site they've been visiting for weeks — a valley where the shimmer
is strongest. The geometry of the valley is beautiful and completely wrong: paths that slope
upward lead downward, the center is somehow farther away the closer you get. Ivo appears, trying
to measure it. Pell finally admits they're scared — that the shimmer they've been chasing means
something is ending, and they didn't want to know that. Ivo, unexpectedly, says: *"Yes. And we're
here anyway."* Wall effect. The valley's geometry briefly settles, then resumes.

### Act 3 — The Facility

The party reaches the Veritas facility. The government agency is there — small team, nervous,
relieved someone finally showed up who might understand what's happening. They grant access to
the sealed wing. They do not have good answers.

The sealed wing is the Fracture level: the geometry here is maximally unstable. Portals, overlapping
spaces, non-Euclidean traversal. It is the most visually spectacular the game has been. It is also
clearly winding down — not escalating. The geometry gets stranger and more beautiful and then, in
moments, briefly perfectly still. Each stillness is a little longer than the last.

Daven explains what Project Meridian did. He explains what the bubble is and what it's doing. He
explains that the bubble will close when it reaches its origin point — the exact moment of the
experiment — and that no one can stop it because it started finishing 17 years ago.

Cole dies here. Quiet, mid-sentence. Whatever connected him to the bubble's boundary dissolves
with it. Daven finishes what Cole was saying. It's a small, private moment in the middle of
everything.

The bubble closes. The geometry of the sealed wing goes completely still — ordinary. Just a room.

The party stands in it for a moment, then walks out.

---

## Ending

The anomalies are gone. The world is fine. Normal.

Pell returns to the valley. The shimmer is gone. They stay for a long time. Ivo stands next to
them and does not try to explain anything.

Sela sits somewhere alone and realizes she's not performing. She isn't sure when that stopped.
It's fine.

Kai stops moving. Sela is next to him. Nothing is said about what they are to each other.

Daven leaves something at the spot where Cole died. He doesn't explain it. He walks back to
the rest of them.

Final image: the four of them on a high point at sunset. The palettes of all four characters
bleed into each other in the light. The camera pulls back. No dialogue.

---

## The Wall Effect

Appears **five times** total, once at the end of each character chapter plus once in Act 1:

1. Act 1 — the inn anomaly (shared, involuntary — establishes the visual language)
2. Kai's chapter — he admits fear
3. Sela's chapter — she chooses herself
4. Daven's chapter — he tells the truth
5. Pell's chapter — they admit the ending

In each case: walls breathe inward for ~8 seconds, color hypersaturates, the world holds still,
then releases. No gameplay interruption. The character continues.

---

## The Fracture Level

The sealed wing of the Veritas facility. Act 3 only. Geometry fully unstable — portals, overlapping
rooms, non-Euclidean traversal. The most visually extreme sequence in the game, and also the last
time the anomalies exist. They are most spectacular right before they stop.

When the bubble closes, the wing returns to ordinary geometry. The contrast is the point.
